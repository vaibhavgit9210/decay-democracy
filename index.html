The Slow Decay of Democracy

Democracies rarely die in a coup anymore — they erode, one institution at a time. Can that erosion be detected in data? We calibrate a detector on three documented cases, check it stays quiet on a healthy control, then point it at India — and reach a verdict.

■ VERDICT — HYPOTHESIS SUPPORTED

Yes. India shows the same decay signature as documented backsliders.

The detector — calibrated on Hungary, Turkey and Venezuela, quiet on Switzerland — fires for India: a fall of −0.33 in the liberal-democracy index from its 1998 peak, sustained for 17 years, onset ≈ 2013, in the classic order (press first, courts last). Details, method and caveats below.

02A decay detector

Slow decay has a measurable shape. Borrowing thresholds from V-Dem's “Episodes of Regime Transformation” research, a country is in a decay episode when:

DETECTOR — fires when, from the country's post-1990 peak of the liberal-democracy index:
① total decline ≥ 0.10  ·  ② the index stays below peak − 0.05 for ≥ 5 consecutive years.
Onset = start of the steepest 10-year fall. Calibration: must fire on Hungary, Turkey, Venezuela; must stay quiet on Switzerland.

Calibration passes: all three known backsliders fire, the control does not. Only then is the detector allowed to look at India.

06The verdict, in full

■ THE SENTENCE UNDER TEST

“India shows the signs of slow democratic decay seen in countries with a documented history of it.”

Verdict: correct — the sentence makes sense and is supported by the data.

  • ✔ The detector fires: −0.333 from the 1998 peak (0.593 → 0.260 in 2025), below threshold for 17 straight years — a larger fall than Turkey's at the same point in its episode.
  • ✔ The sequence matches the backslider playbook: expression/media first (2014, −0.44), civil liberties next (2014, −0.23), association (2015, −0.23), judiciary last and least (2019, −0.16).
  • ✔ Aligned at onset, India's curve shadows Hungary's — the canonical slow-decay case.
  • ✔ The control (Switzerland) stays quiet, so the detector isn't just flagging everything.

Honest caveats — what the verdict does not say:

  • One family of sources. V-Dem is expert-coded (point estimates carry uncertainty bands). It is, however, corroborated: Freedom House cut India to “Partly Free” (2021) and V-Dem has classified India as an “electoral autocracy” since its 2021 report.
  • Decay ≠ destiny. The index measures the past, not the future. India's judiciary still scores 0.667 — closer to Hungary's than to Venezuela's collapsed 0.017 — and elections remain genuinely competitive (2024 returned a coalition, and the series shows a small 2024 uptick before a new low in 2025).
  • Reversals happen. Poland (2023–) and Brazil show backsliding can be stopped and partially undone while elections still function. That door is measurably still open in India's data.
  • “Slow” is the point. No single year looks like a rupture — the average annual drop is ~0.02. That is exactly why decay is detected with a decade-scale window, and why it is easy to deny in the moment.
